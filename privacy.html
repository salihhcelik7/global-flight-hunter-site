1. Who we are

MIA GO ("the app", "we") is a flight discovery application for the Turkish market. It shows observed flight prices and tells you when a price is unusual compared with that route's own history.

2. The short version

  • No accounts. There is no sign-up, no login, no password, no email field anywhere in the app or the API.
  • No advertising or analytics SDKs. None are present in the mobile app's dependencies.
  • No location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or calendar access. The app requests exactly three Android permissions: internet, notifications, and vibration. It requests no iOS privacy permission other than notifications.
  • Your searches are not stored. Not the form fields, not the free-text query, not in the database and not in the logs.

3. What we collect, and why

3.1 Data you give us deliberately

Price alerts. If you create an alert, we store the rule on our server: the origin airport, the destination airport (or "anywhere"), your price ceiling and/or minimum discount percentage, your date range, date flexibility, minimum data freshness and currency, plus timestamps of when it was created and last evaluated. We store this so we can check it against new observations.

We also store what the alert matched: route, dates, price, comparison baseline, discount percentage, source and observation time.

LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED: An alert rule reveals travel intent (where someone plans to go and when). Confirm whether that alone requires anything beyond ordinary personal data treatment under KVKK — in particular whether any combination of origin/destination could be argued to reveal a special category of data (for example a route habitually associated with pilgrimage or medical travel), and whether the honest answer is simply "no, we do not infer anything from it".

CODE GAP — must be fixed before launch, and this clause must be rewritten to match whatever is built: alerts are currently stored without any device or user identifier, and the alert listing endpoint returns all alerts to any caller. As written today, one person's alert would be visible to another. This policy must not be published while that is true. See F10-DATA-INVENTORY.md §4 Finding A.

Device push token. If — and only if — you grant notification permission, your device obtains a push token from Expo and the app sends it to our server together with your platform (ios/android/web), your app language, and the fact that permission was granted. We store this so we have an address to send a price alert to. If you refuse notification permission, no token exists and nothing is sent.

A push token is not your name or your email, but it is unique to one installation on one device, so we treat it as personal data.

3.2 Data the app keeps on your device only

Your preferences — home airports, currency, language, theme, and your search defaults (optimization mode, maximum stops, baggage requirement, self-transfer tolerance, risk ordering) — are stored in the app's own local storage on your phone. They are never uploaded. Saved routes and the in-app candidate cache exist only in memory and are gone when you close the app.

Deleting the app deletes all of this.

3.3 Data we do NOT collect

We do not collect: your name, email address, phone number, postal address, date of birth, payment details, precise or coarse location, contacts, photos, health data, financial data, advertising identifiers (IDFA / Android Advertising ID), or any other data used to track you across apps or websites.

We do not build a profile of you. We do not run automated decision-making that produces legal effects concerning you.

3.4 Searches

When you search, your input — origin, destination, dates, passenger counts, cabin class, and any free text you type into the AI search box — is used to run that search and then discarded. It is not written to our database and it is not written to our logs. Free text is interpreted by a rule-based parser that runs on our own server; it is not sent to any third-party AI service.

3.5 Price observations

Our price history is collected by a background process that scans a fixed list of routes on a schedule. It is a record of what flights cost, not a record of what anyone searched for. Nothing in it is connected to you.

3.6 Server logs

Our application writes one log line per request containing a randomly generated request identifier, the URL path, the HTTP method, the response status, how long it took, and a coarse endpoint category. It does not log your IP address, your device's user agent, or the contents of your request. Credentials and push tokens are stripped from every log line automatically.

Our hosting provider, like every web host, may keep its own access logs that include IP addresses.

Every period in that table is enforced by RetentionService (app/db/retention.py) and the table in DATA-RETENTION-POLICY.md §2 is generated from the same constants, so the published number and the deleting number cannot drift apart. A configuration override may only ever shorten a period; a longer value is refused and logged.

Two gaps remain and are stated as gaps in DATA-RETENTION-POLICY.md §4.2: alerts that have expired are marked expired rather than deleted (the table has no expired_at column to delete them by), and no backup policy has been decided because no hosting decision has been made.

6. Who we share data with

We do not sell data. We do not share data for advertising. The complete list of third parties that receive anything at all:

Travelpayouts (Aviasales Data API) — our server asks Travelpayouts for prices on a route and dates. It sends the route, the dates, the currency, the market code (tr) and our own API token. It does not send your IP address, your device information, your push token, or anything identifying you. Your device never contacts Travelpayouts directly.

LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED: Read the Travelpayouts partner terms and confirm (a) what they permit us to say publicly about the data relationship, (b) whether they are a processor or an independent controller here, and (c) what the affiliate programme requires us to disclose to users about commissions.

Expo push service — when a price alert fires, our server would send the notification content (route, prices, date, deep link) and your push token to Expo, which delivers it to Apple's or Google's push infrastructure.

Note on current state: push delivery is switched off. The configured notifier is a no-op that records that it sent nothing. No notification has ever been delivered. This paragraph describes what happens when the feature is enabled, which is why it is written in the conditional.

OpenFreeMap — when you open the map screen, your device requests map tiles directly from tiles.openfreemap.org. Like any web request, that server sees your IP address and which tiles you asked for. We do not control that server and we do not receive anything from it. No account, key or identifier of ours is involved.

LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED: Confirm whether OpenFreeMap's own terms and privacy stance need to be linked from here, and whether a direct device-to-third-party request that exposes the user's IP requires prior consent (a cookie/tracker-style consent question rather than a purely informational disclosure) under KVKK or ePrivacy for EU users.

Nobody else. Duffel and Skyscanner adapters exist in our codebase but are not enabled and receive nothing.

Not a third party: our typeface. The app is set in Poppins. The font files are bundled inside the application package and are read from local storage. No request is made to Google Fonts or any other font host, so no font request carries your IP address anywhere. This is worth stating because a web font loaded from a CDN would be exactly such a request, and many apps have one.

We may disclose data if required by a lawful order.

8. Your rights

Under GDPR (if you are in the EEA/UK) and KVKK Art. 11 (if you are in Turkey) you can ask us to tell you what we hold about you, correct it, delete it, restrict or object to processing, and receive it in a portable form. You can withdraw consent for notifications at any time — either in your phone's settings or by turning notifications off in the app.

How to exercise them, honestly stated: because we hold no account, we cannot look you up by name or email. Your identifier is an installation id generated on your device. What you can do:

  • Erase everything we hold for your installation — from inside the app. It deletes your alerts and everything attached to them, your device's push token records, and the delivery records for those tokens. There is no account to close, so this is the deletion right.
  • Delete a single alert — from inside the app.
  • Everything on your device — deleting the app removes it, and it is not copied anywhere first: Android's automatic backup is switched off for this app (android.allowBackup: false). It was on by default, which would have left a copy of your preferences in your Google account after you deleted the app — and this sentence would have been false.

If you send a request we cannot connect to any record, we will say so rather than pretend to have acted.

How we know it works. When erasure was first implemented it deleted the alerts and the token records but not the delivery records: it looked them up by the raw push token, while that column has only ever held a one-way fingerprint of it. The delete matched nothing and reported success. It is fixed, and the test that covers it now records data the way production records it — which is what the earlier test failed to do.

LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED: Confirm the statutory response deadline to quote (KVKK: 30 days; GDPR: one month) and the complaint routes to name — the Turkish Data Protection Authority (KVKK Kurumu) and, for EU users, the relevant supervisory authority.

Traffic between the app and our server uses HTTPS.
